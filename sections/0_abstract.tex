\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The Internet forwards packets without regard for why they were sent, so the network optimizes a target
that rarely matches what an application values. Path-aware architectures such as SCION let endpoints
choose among multiple end-to-end paths, placing that decision with the party that knows the
application's intent. This thesis asks how to make it well, at two granularities: choosing one path for
general flows, and, for real-time video, jointly choosing how much traffic each path carries
\emph{and} what the application produces.

The strongest result is that the two decisions cannot be made well apart. A two-timescale pair of
reinforcement-learning agents co-optimizing a video call's encoding bitrate and its per-path split over
Multipath QUIC beats all eight comparison points under all three application intents --- among them
minRTT and a WebRTC-style deployed stack --- leading the closest of them, that WebRTC-style stack, by
0.065 to 0.143 QoE, three to seven times the spread between training runs; removing the transport agent
forfeits most of the achievable quality and removing the application agent a fifth of it. What makes this
possible is architectural: replacing the permutation-equivariant head that scores every path with shared
weights by a fixed-dimension one costs 0.231 QoE and drops the system from first in nine of nine
evaluation cells to none, where the shared-weight policy runs unmodified from two paths to twelve
at a fixed parameter count. The same architecture carries the single-path study, raising residual
bottleneck capacity by 41\% at under half the parameter count, on one probe per decision instead of
thirty.

Intent conditioning is the thread joining the two, and it comes with a boundary. \emph{Where} the
conditioning enters decides whether it acts at all: a signal reaching only the terms shared by every
candidate cancels under the comparison that selects a path --- exactly, and on the whole action, when
that comparison is a softmax. Routed into the per-candidate comparison, one policy serves intents it
never trained on and replaces three separately trained specialists at a third of the cost. But three
mechanisms in an ordinary architecture violate that premise, so a trained encoder routes intent past a
cancellation the theory says should stop it: the principle identifies which
routings carry a guarantee, not which happen to work.

What governs the payoff of joint control is not whether the network is moving but how unevenly capacity
is spread across paths and how strictly the application prices delay --- and that map bounds our
headline claim: it holds on a milder held-out network and fails on a harsher one. Transferred zero-shot
to a packet-level backend, only three of six independently trained policies keep their ranking, and
surrogate score does not predict which; trained on that backend instead, the pair leads every
comparison point in nine of nine cells and beats transfer by 0.228 QoE. A cheap surrogate is therefore
useless for choosing among the policies it produces, and not free to train in either. Throughout, the
single-path study runs on a generated SCION topology; the multipath study assumes only path
multiplicity.
