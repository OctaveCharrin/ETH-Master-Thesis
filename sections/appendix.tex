\section{Single-Path Selection: Agent Hyperparameters}
\label{app:p1hyper}

\Cref{tab:app:p1hyper} lists the hyperparameters shared by the flat, scoring, and conditional agents of
\cref{sec:p1impl:agents}; the flat agent differs only in training for 533~episodes rather than 666.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Hyperparameters for the single-path selection agents (\cref{ch:single_path}).}
  \label{tab:app:p1hyper}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    Parameter & Value \\
    \midrule
    Hidden layers / width          & 2 / 128 \\
    Learning rate (Adam)           & $10^{-3}$ \\
    Discount $\gamma$              & 0.95 \\
    Batch size                     & 32 \\
    Replay capacity                & $10^4$ (minimum 500 before learning) \\
    Target update                  & soft (Polyak), $\tau = 0.05$, every 100 gradient steps \\
    Prioritized replay             & $\alpha = 0.6$, $\beta$ annealed $0.4 \rightarrow 1.0$ \\
    Exploration                    & $\varepsilon$-greedy, $1.0 \rightarrow 0.01$, decay 0.995/episode \\
    Dropout                        & 0.1 \\
    Gradient clipping              & 1.0 \\
    Episodes $\times$ steps        & $666 \times 24$ (flat: $533 \times 24$) \\
    Gradient step frequency        & every 4 environment steps \\
    Invalid-action mask value      & $-10^{9}$ \\
    Random seeds                   & 5 (all agents) \\
    Wall-clock per agent           & 1--3\,min (22-core CPU, no GPU) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two caveats on this table. The $\varepsilon$ schedule is stated as its configured range, but a run is too
short to traverse it: at 0.995 decay per episode, $\varepsilon$ reaches only 0.036 after 666~episodes and
0.069 after 533, so the 0.01~floor is never attained and all reported agents retain a few percent of
exploration at the end of training. Seeding fixes network initialization, prioritized-replay sampling,
and $\varepsilon$-greedy exploration; the environment's pair, hour, and profile streams are deliberately
left fixed across seeds, so every seed sees the identical stream of training contexts and only the
learning process varies.

The non-stationary mechanisms are hazards converted per frame from per-second rates. Path churn takes a
live path down at \SI{0.08}{\per\second} and revives a dead one at \SI{0.30}{\per\second}, with at least
three of six paths live at all times. Regime shifts resample a path's capacity multiplier uniformly in
$[0.55, 1.25]$ at \SI{0.20}{\per\second}. Congestion bursts drop capacity to 45\% for
\SI{0.5}{\second} at \SI{0.10}{\per\second}. The correlated-failure group $\{5, 6\}$ drops to 50\% for
\SI{1.2}{\second} at \SI{0.05}{\per\second}.

\paragraph{Intent conditioning.} The conditioned agents of \cref{sec:p2eval:conditioning} append a
five-component intent vector to the observation: the delay, jitter, and loss weights $(b, c, d/2)$ of
\cref{eq:p2qoe}, the frame deadline, and the latency normalizer, the last two scaled by
\SI{800}{\milli\second}. The existing observation features are left untouched and the normalizer is
disclosed as an input rather than fixed across intents, which is what keeps the per-intent specialists
of \cref{tab:p2transfer} a valid baseline against the conditioned policy --- and is also the third of
the three premise violations reported in \cref{sec:p2design:cancel}, since that normalizer divides a
per-path feature. Two conditioning modes are compared: \texttt{global}, which supplies the vector to the
global context slot alone, and \texttt{perpath}, which additionally broadcasts it to every per-path
feature row, adding $5 \times 6 = 30$ input dimensions. Training randomizes the intent per episode,
stratified over the three anchors of \cref{tab:p2profiles} plus a continuously sampled interior; the
randomization is training-only and evaluation never resamples. Both arms are trained for six seeds at the
budget of \cref{tab:app:p2hyper}. Source: \texttt{rl-mpquic/configs/dynamic\_intent\{,\_glob\}.yaml}.

\section{Supplementary Results}
\label{app:extra}

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{p1app_training_curves.png}
  \caption{Training curves for the single-path agents of \cref{sec:p1impl:agents}: episode reward
  (25-episode moving average, left) and training loss (right) over the 533 or 666~episodes each agent was
  trained for. The four path-scoring agents converge to within 0.01 of one another at roughly $+0.81$,
  while the flat DQN plateaus near $+0.05$ with a \emph{rising} loss despite the identical budget, data,
  and optimizer -- the evidence that its deficit is architectural rather than a matter of training time.
  The simple scoring agent's loss also diverges, which is why the enhanced variant is used throughout.}
  \label{fig:app:training}

\Cref{fig:app:training} substantiates the convergence discussion of \cref{sec:p1impl:agents}. The
per-episode statistics also carry the stratified schedule's profile label for each episode, giving
roughly 111~episodes per intent for the conditional agents; these per-intent curves are the only place
the two probe-frugality profiles of \cref{tab:app:profiles} appear, since \cref{sec:p1eval:setup}
excludes them from the conditioning studies.
